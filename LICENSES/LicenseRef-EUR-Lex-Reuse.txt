EUR-Lex reuse terms (Commission Decision 2011/833/EU)
=====================================================

This identifier covers the EU legal acts vendored under `docs/law/eu/`, fetched
from the EU Publications Office by `scripts/vendor/law-eu.sh` (the EUR-Lex
corpus, over the CELEX content-negotiation URI). It is not an SPDX-listed licence.
It is the reuse policy the publisher states for those documents, recorded in
machine-readable form so that a glob elsewhere in REUSE.toml cannot assert the
wrong terms over them.

What the publisher states
-------------------------

https://eur-lex.europa.eu/content/legal-notice/legal-notice.html, read
2026-09-12 and re-read unchanged 2026-10-05:

How it applies to this repository
---------------------------------

The vendored files are legal documents published in EUR-Lex, reused unmodified.
The source is acknowledged in each act's `PROVENANCE.md` and in the index at
`docs/law/README.md`, and no change has been made to any of them: they are the
publisher's own bytes, with a SHA-256 recorded so that stays checkable.

Three of the vendored acts are pinned at a CONSOLIDATED text (the GDPR, the
MDR and the ePrivacy Directive). Consolidation is the EU's own editorial work,
which the notice above licenses under CC BY 4.0, so each of those three
`text.html` files is declared in REUSE.toml as this identifier AND
`CC-BY-4.0`, naming both sets of obligations rather than picking one. The OJ
documents vendored beside them (the GDPR as published with its recitals and
its 2018 corrigendum; the ePrivacy Directive as published and Directive
2009/136/EC) are not consolidated texts and carry this identifier alone. The
full CC BY 4.0 text is at `LICENSES/CC-BY-4.0.txt`.

Nothing in this repository claims any right in these documents. No EU logo is
reproduced, and the EUR-Lex metadata that would be CC0 is not vendored.
