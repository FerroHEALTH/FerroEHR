- An AQL query that reads values along a path no longer spends up to a second compiling itself before it runs. Each path step was bound through a set-returning function that PostgreSQL estimates at ten rows, so a chain of steps multiplied the row estimate tenfold per step: the planner chose join orders for a statement millions of times larger than the real one and switched on JIT compilation for it. On the storage bench, a wide projection of 9 values over one EHR read in 1 s instead of 15 to 20 ms, and an ordered WHERE on a magnitude in about 0.5 s instead of 50 ms (#4430). Each step is now a `LATERAL` subquery that the planner estimates from its own plan; results do not change.
