- Operational policy has a home in the party store, with no configuration key. FerroEHR ships example policy definitions: a system's `AGENT` (`openEHR-DEMOGRAPHIC-AGENT.system.v1`), the acts-for `PARTY_RELATIONSHIP` to the provider it acts for, and the system-client, federation-partner and privileged `ROLE`s, each with a `CAPABILITY` whose credentials hold the grant. The binary carries a default template of each, and each boot stores one only when no definition of its id is stored, never overwriting a deployment's version. A party is a policy party by the codes it holds, in any archetype, as the entry under Changed describes. Every write of a policy party is validated against its own template. Only a caller holding `[authz.rbac] admin_role` writes or reads a policy party through the Demographic API, a `CONTRIBUTION` included; any other caller, a SCIM client too, is answered `403`. The book's "Operational policy" page documents the terminology as the contract, with a worked example of each kind.
- The default demographic set gains a patient `PERSON` (`deploy/definitions/patient/`), adapted from the openEHR CKM `person-patient` archetype, with its patient identifiers, date of birth and sex in `details`, its names named by their usage, a postal and an electronic address, and its relationships to personal contacts, payers and providers, and a healthcare provider `ORGANISATION` (`deploy/definitions/provider/`), identified by its provider identifier, which the acts-for relationship targets and a federation partner is. The quickstart, the hosted sandbox, the conformance harness, the viewer lane and the Helm chart's default `definitions.files` load all seven definitions, and every release attaches each.
