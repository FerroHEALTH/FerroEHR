- Operational policy has a home in the party store: FerroEHR ships the policy definitions under `deploy/definitions/policy/` (a system's `AGENT`, the acts-for `PARTY_RELATIONSHIP` to the provider it acts for, and the system-client, data-migration, federation-partner and privileged `ROLE`s, each with a `CAPABILITY` whose credentials hold the grant), and maps them with `[authz.party_binding.agent]`, `[authz.party_binding.acts_for]` and `[authz.party_binding.system_client_role]`, `data_migration_role`, `federation_partner_role` and `privileged_role`. Boot checks each definition is stored and rooted at its class, and every write of a policy party is validated against it. Only a caller holding `[authz.rbac] admin_role` writes or reads a policy party through the Demographic API, a `CONTRIBUTION` included; any other caller, a SCIM client too, is answered `403` naming the template. The server does not yet read a grant from these parties. The book's new "Operational policy" page describes the model with a worked example of each kind.
- The default demographic set gains a patient `PERSON` (`deploy/definitions/patient/`), adapted from the openEHR CKM `person-patient` archetype, with its patient identifiers, date of birth and sex in `details`, its names named by their usage, a postal and an electronic address, and its relationships to personal contacts, payers and providers, and a healthcare provider `ORGANISATION` (`deploy/definitions/provider/`), identified by its provider identifier, which the acts-for relationship targets and a federation partner is. The quickstart, the hosted sandbox, the conformance harness, the viewer lane and the Helm chart's default `definitions.files` load all thirteen definitions, and every release attaches each.
