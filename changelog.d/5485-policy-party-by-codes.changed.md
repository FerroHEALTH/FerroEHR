- A policy party is recognised by what it holds, in any archetype or template, and no longer by its archetype id: a `CAPABILITY` whose `credentials` hold a `DV_CODED_TEXT` of FerroEHR's operation terminology `ferroehr-operations`, a `PARTY_RELATIONSHIP` whose `name` or `details` hold `ferroehr-operations::acts_for`, or a `DV_IDENTIFIER` of type `client_id`. A deployment's own `ROLE` archetype whose capability holds `import_ehr` is a policy party, and a party of the shipped policy archetypes that holds none of these codes is an ordinary one. The shipped policy definitions are examples that carry the codes: each policy `ROLE` requires an `Operation` element (`id90`) in its credentials, and the acts-for relationship an acts-for code element (`id6`) in its details. A `403` for a policy party now names what makes it one in place of the archetype. The book's "Operational policy" page states the rule.
