- A write's access record is no longer lost when a server stops between the write's commit and its record (#5762). The audit store lives in its own cluster, so a write's records are written first, held under a reservation that the write's own transaction commits, and released by the chainer's next round in the transaction that chains them, so a write costs two commits. When a replica stops in between, the reconciler of any other replica releases those records two minutes later, exactly as they were written and once, and discards the held records of a write that never committed. The `audit_sender` health indicator and the new `audit_reservations_held`, `audit_reservation_age_seconds` and `audit_reservations_reconciled_total` metrics show reservations waiting for it. See the book's "Audit trail" page, "How a record is written".
