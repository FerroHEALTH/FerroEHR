- Breaking: own access comes from the party binding alone, for every issuer kind. FerroEHR moves to `ferrohealth-smart` 0.31, whose `ferrosmart` preset reads a FerroSMART token as the patient's own when its `fhirUser` is `Patient/<x>`, its `patient` claim is `x` and its scopes are `patient/` alone. That reading is a check and never grants. Such a token's login is looked up in FerroSMART's `[[auth.party]]` table like any person's and has to bind a `PERSON` with no professional `ROLE` and no representation; otherwise the request is refused `403`, with `refused-unbound` (or `refused-key-claim-missing`) when the login binds no `PERSON` and `refused-wrong-marker` when it binds a professional's or a representative's. A `patients` issuer's token is the patient themself only when its login is a binding key of the `PERSON` its launch EHR's `EHR_STATUS.subject` names. A login no `PERSON` carries, and a token with no `ehrId`, used to be read as the patient and are now refused `403` with `refused-unbound`, so each patient of a `patients` issuer needs their login as a key on their `PERSON`. A caller bound as the person themself whose token names an `ehrId` is refused `403` with `refused-representation-unrecorded` when that EHR's subject is another `PERSON`. FerroEHR cannot map a FHIR `Patient` id to a `PERSON` without the FHIR pairing, so it does not compare the `patient` claim. `[auth.smart] patient_issuers` keeps only the trust it names, that an issuer's `patient/` grants count, and never makes a caller the record's person. The access record of the record's person names their `PERSON` for both issuer kinds. A `PERSON` whose login is a portal key and that is the EHR's subject, while `inactive` or `abandoned`, is refused `refused-inactive` (#6080).
- Dependencies: `ferrohealth-smart` 0.31.0, `ferrohealth-session` 0.26.0, `ferrobridge-term` 0.15.0 and `ihe-iti` 0.16.0, with one `ferrohealth-smart` in the lock (#6080).
