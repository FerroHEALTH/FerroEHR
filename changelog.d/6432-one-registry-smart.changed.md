- **Breaking: FerroEHR reads the caller from the token's standard claims and keeps no client list of its own.** FerroEHR moves to `ferrohealth-smart` 0.34 and `ferrohealth-session` 0.29. Trusting FerroSMART, or any SMART App Launch 2.2 authorization server, takes one table naming its `issuer`; `audience`, `role_claims`, `party`, `attributes`, `assurance` and `sender_constraint` stay optional. A token whose `aud` names FerroEHR admits its client, since the authorization server's registry is the one list of clients (RFC 9068 §4). A user is read by the type of their `fhirUser` (`Patient`, `Practitioner`, `PractitionerRole`, `RelatedPerson`, `Person`, or a user of no stated type), a client credentials token by its `sub` equal to its `client_id` (RFC 9068 §2.2), and an RFC 8693 `act` names the actor, recorded as the delegated acting mode. A login is bound by its token's `sub`. A patient's own access comes from their token bound to their `PERSON`; a representative is a `RelatedPerson`, or a user whose `PERSON` carries the representative marker alone, admitted for a subject the CDR stores a live representation of. Every trusted issuer's `patient` claim is its launch context, a `patient/` grant reaches only the EHR its token's `ehrId` names (SMART on openEHR master08 §Resource Scopes), so the person's own records by identity alone need `user/` grants, and `[authz.party_binding] provider` is one provider.
- To move a deployment: drop `kind` (keep `kind = "federation"`), `preset`, `profile_file`, the vendor profile keys and `[auth.idp]` from each issuer, drop the `sign_in_persons`, `act_for_persons` and `access_without_scope` grants and the guest admissions, and make the identity provider put `client_id` in every access token and the client's id in a client credentials token's `sub`. The book's "Authentication & access" page has the keys.
