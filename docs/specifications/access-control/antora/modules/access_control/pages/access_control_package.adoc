= The access_control Package

include::partial$draft_notice.adoc[]

== Overview

A `CONFIDENTIALITY_SETTINGS` is the `_settings_` of one `EHR_ACCESS`, and `EHR_ACCESS.scheme()` returns `confidentiality` for it. It holds the controller's facts about that EHR: the confidentiality of the whole record, as a `CONFIDENTIALITY_MARKING`, and the named parties excluded from it. `ACCESS_DIRECTIVE_SETTINGS` inherits it and is the scheme `access_directives`: it adds the default disposition of the EHR, the gate-keepers, the `ACCESS_DIRECTIVE` instances the subject gave and the `ACCESS_LABEL` instances on single objects. `EHR_ACCESS._settings_` is 0..1 (RM EHR IM, `EHR_ACCESS`), so an EHR holds one settings object, and the directive scheme carries the controller's facts with it. An `ACCESS_POLICY`, kept in a `VERSIONED_ACCESS_POLICY`, holds the controller's facts for every EHR it keeps: its own rules, the restrictions of the access log's subject view, and the facts about care relationships and representation. `ACCESS_ACTOR_SCOPE` and `ACCESS_DATA_SCOPE` describe callers and data, and are used by directives, labels, classifications and rules alike. Care relationships, memberships and representations are coded `PARTY_RELATIONSHIP` instances, each stored on its own in a `VERSIONED_PARTY_RELATIONSHIP`.

----
ACCESS_CONTROL_SETTINGS (RM ehr, abstract)
  └─ CONFIDENTIALITY_SETTINGS          scheme "confidentiality": marking, exclusions
       └─ ACCESS_DIRECTIVE_SETTINGS    scheme "access_directives": default_access, gate_keepers, directives, labels
----

A system that implements only `CONFIDENTIALITY_SETTINGS` interoperates with one that implements the directives for the part they share.

== Design

=== Confidentiality of a Record

The Security Policy names "malicious theft of or access to health data (e.g. of a celebrity or politician)" as a threat (xref:BASE:architecture_overview:security.adoc[Security and Confidentiality], Threats to Security and Privacy), and the HL7 confidentiality codes give the levels for it: `R` restricted, whose examples include "a patient's standing as an employee or a celebrity", and `V` very restricted, for example "information about a victim of abuse" (`HL7_Confidentiality` 5.0.0). `CONFIDENTIALITY_SETTINGS._marking_` puts one level on the whole record, with `_set_by_`, `_set_at_` and a `_reason_` written for the subject to read. The marking has its own `_set_by_` and `_set_at_` because `EHR_ACCESS` is versioned: a later version committed by another party for another reason, such as the subject adding a directive, carries the marking forward unchanged, and the marking keeps who set it. An explicit `N` records a deliberate reset with its reason; a void marking also reads as `N`.

The levels are ordered `N` < `R` < `V` (xref:terminology.adoc#_codes_from_other_terminologies[Terminology]), so a permit that names a level clears the levels below it. The confidentiality of a datum is the highest of the marking of its EHR, the marking of an `ACCESS_LABEL` on its object, and the confidentiality the policy's classifications give its kind, and the procedure decides on that one level at step 6 (xref:decision_procedure.adoc[The Decision Procedure]).

=== Exclusions

`CONFIDENTIALITY_SETTINGS._exclusions_` holds denies the controller sets for this EHR alone, each an `ACCESS_DIRECTIVE` of kind `exclusion` with the effect `deny`, naming the parties it keeps out by `_applies_to_` (by `party` or by `relationship`), with `_instructed_by_`, `_time_validity_`, `_legal_grounds_` and a reason in `_details_`. An exclusion keeps a named person out of one record whatever else admits them: the parent whose authority stands and who must still be kept out, or a professional with a known conflict. It binds a representative and a gate-keeper, never the subject, and only the controller reads and writes it. A national law that lets a provider decline to treat a person as a representative, for example in a case of abuse or endangerment, is carried by an exclusion.

=== Care Relationships

The Security Policy limits access by relevance: "the overriding principle of access control must be 'relevance' both in terms of user identity (who is delivering care to the patient) and time" (xref:BASE:architecture_overview:security.adoc[Security and Confidentiality], Security Policy, Access list), and puts the evaluation of a care deliverer "in each care delivery environment, not in the EHR" (The Problem of Roles). Regulation (EU) 2025/327 Art 11(1) gives health professionals access to the data "of natural persons under their treatment", and defines neither the treatment nor a professional's part in it. The package records that fact as a care relationship the care delivery environment writes, and reads it through a closed set of paths (xref:decision_procedure.adoc#_holding_a_care_relationship[Holding a Care Relationship]).

A care relationship is a `PARTY_RELATIONSHIP` (RM Demographic IM, Party Relationships) whose `_name_` is the code `under_care_of` (invariant `type = name`), whose `_source_` is the subject of care and whose `_target_` is the carer: a `PERSON`, a `ROLE` a professional performs, a `GROUP` (a care team) or an `ORGANISATION` unit. Its `_time_validity_` is the period of the care: an admission, an episode, a registration with a practice, a consultation, a period of cover. Its `_details_` hold elements named by the codes of the `care relationship element` group:

[cols="2,1,2,4", options="header"]
|===
|Element |Occurrences |Value |Meaning

|`care_role`
|1..1
|`DV_CODED_TEXT`, of the value set bound for `care_role`
|The part the target plays in the care of the subject. A writer that knows no role writes `HL7_ParticipationType::PART`.

|`provider`
|1..1
|`DV_IDENTIFIER`
|The healthcare provider in whose care the relationship is, an `ORGANISATION`: `_issuer_` is its reference's namespace, `_id_` its id and `_type_` `ORGANISATION`. A caller holds the relationship only while it acts for that provider or one of its units. A deployment with one provider writes the same value every time.

|`covers_for`
|0..1
|`DV_IDENTIFIER`
|The carer the target replaces for the period of this relationship, in the same form; never the target itself.

|`episode`
|0..1
|`DV_IDENTIFIER`
|The episode of care the relationship is scoped to, by the `_issuer_` and `_id_` the care delivery environment gives it (<<Episodes of Care>>). A relationship without it is not scoped to an episode.

|`basis`
|0..1
|`DV_CODED_TEXT`, of the `care basis` group
|`assigned` (the default when absent): written by the care delivery environment. `self_declared`: written by the professional it names, admitted only where the policy allows it.
|===

A relationship is ended by closing `_time_validity_`, never by removal, so a grace period can apply and the history stays readable. `_time_validity_` is `DV_INTERVAL<DV_DATE>`, so a relationship is bounded in days, read in the policy's time zone. Cover is a relationship of its own: the covering professional holds a relationship of the role they cover, for the period of the cover, with `covers_for` naming the professional they replace (The Problem of Roles: "temporary replacements due to illness or holiday"). Whole-practice cover is a membership of the practice for the period. A night or weekend shift is who is on duty, which the rostering system keeps current as team membership.

A *membership* is a `PARTY_RELATIONSHIP` named `member_of` whose `_source_` is the member (a professional's `PERSON`, or a `GROUP` or unit that is part of another) and whose `_target_` is the `GROUP` or `ORGANISATION` unit, with its own `_time_validity_`. A membership may also come from the caller's verified credential, as a group claim the system documents.

A *representation* is a `PARTY_RELATIONSHIP` named `represents` whose `_source_` acts for its `_target_`, the subject. Its `_details_` hold `basis` (1..1, `DV_CODED_TEXT` of the value set bound for `representation_basis`), why the source may act for the subject, `provider` (1..1, `DV_IDENTIFIER`, as for a care relationship), and `evidence` (0..1, `DV_IDENTIFIER`), the court order, register entry or written authorisation; its `_time_validity_` is the period of the representation. The writer decides that the source represents the subject, under the law that applies to it, and stores the fact; the system decides a representative's access on that fact and records it, and never derives a representation from an age, a capacity or a legal-basis table (<<The Representation as a Stored Fact>>).

A *kinship* completes the core: a `related_to` relationship records a kinship between two persons: `kinship` (1..1, `DV_CODED_TEXT` of the value set bound for `kinship`, by default the openEHR Terminology group `subject relationship`). Kinship never grants access. It can refuse it, as a conflict of interest (step 5 of the procedure).

An archetype may constrain any of these relationships; the procedure reads the codes of `_name_` and of each element's `_name_`, never an archetype identifier or a path.

=== Episodes of Care

The Security Policy limits access in time to "the current episode of care, and for some reasonable, limited time afterward" and records an episode's start and end as `ADMIN_ENTRY` instances (xref:BASE:architecture_overview:security.adoc[Security and Confidentiality], Security Policy). The EHR Information Model calls "an episode in a hospital" one healthcare event an `EVENT_CONTEXT` documents (RM EHR IM, Event Context), and keeps persistent compositions, such as the problem list and the medication list, as "a single source-of-truth over an entire patient lifetime" (Persistent Compositions). Neither says which data belong to which episode, or that a professional's access may be limited to one episode. Regulation (EU) 2025/327 Art 11(1) limits a professional's access to "the relevant and necessary" data of persons under their treatment. This section fills that silence.

* *Identity.* An episode of care is identified by the `DV_IDENTIFIER` the care delivery environment gives it, compared by `_issuer_` and `_id_`; `_type_` is not compared. FHIR `EpisodeOfCare.identifier` and an HL7 v2 visit number are such identifiers. An episode is matched only within one subject. Its status and period belong to the care delivery environment, which states them through the relationships it writes: an episode is open for a carer while the carer's relationship to it is live.
* *Placement.* A composition is placed in an episode by an `ELEMENT` named by the code `episode` of the `record placement element` group anywhere in its `EVENT_CONTEXT._other_context_`, whose value is the episode's `DV_IDENTIFIER`. A composition may name several episodes, such as a consultation note that serves two. A composition with no such element is placed in no episode, and so are an `EHR_STATUS`, a `FOLDER` and an `EHR_ACCESS`. A later version that names other episodes moves the composition, and every version follows the placement of the current one.
* *Persistent compositions.* A composition whose `_category_` is `persistent` (`openehr::431`) is placed in no episode; one that names an episode is refused at commit.
* *Declared episodes.* Every episode a composition names is the `episode` of some care relationship of the subject, live or ended; a composition that names another is refused at commit.
* *Episode relationship.* A care relationship with an `episode` element is scoped to that episode. Its dates, grace, role, path, provider and cover are read as for any care relationship. A relationship *covers* a datum as xref:decision_procedure.adoc#_holding_a_care_relationship[Holding a Care Relationship] defines, so an episode relationship only narrows what the same relationship without an episode admits.

The placement element, the rule for persistent compositions, the declared-episode rule and coverage are this proposal's own. A folder per episode (RM EHR IM, Folders: the directory "might thus be used to represent episodes") stays a classification of references and decides nothing.

=== The Relationship Store

The normative unit is the single relationship. Each care relationship, membership, representation and subject fact is its own versioned object, a `VERSIONED_PARTY_RELATIONSHIP`, committed in contributions with its own `AUDIT_DETAILS`, so every change to it is audit-trailed with who made it (Security Policy, Audit trailing) and a hospital patient's relationships do not rewrite one another. A relationship is addressed by its uid and by the reference of its source, so a system without a demographic service holds relationships whose `_source_` and `_target_` are `PARTY_REF`s in any namespace, such as the `EHR_STATUS._subject_` reference. A system with a demographic service MAY also show a party's relationships in `PARTY._relationships_`, read from the same store. A relationship past its end, its grace and the access log's review age may leave the current set; its history stays.

Beside each care relationship written without an end, the store keeps the end the relationship took from `ACCESS_POLICY._relationship_validity_` when it was committed, with the identifier of the policy version that gave it (xref:decision_procedure.adoc#_writing_relationships[Writing Relationships]). The kept end is a fact of the store, not of the relationship: the committed version carries no end, and the kept end moves only when a new version of the relationship is committed. A rebuild of the facts derived from the relationships keeps it and never computes it again.

A conforming system states which of two levels it meets: *relationship store* (the relationships, addressed by subject reference) or *demographic* (the same, also shown in the parties of a demographic service).

=== The Care Path in a Rule

`ACCESS_ACTOR_SCOPE._relationship_` `care` names the callers that hold a live care relationship to the subject. `_care_roles_` narrows it to relationships in one of the roles listed, and `_care_path_` to one path: `direct`, `team`, `self_declared` or `attested`. A rule of the controller can then say "only the responsible practitioner signs the discharge summary" (a deny with a permit exception whose actor scope names `RESP` and `direct`), or "a record at `V` is read only by the responsible or attending practitioner, directly, for treatment" (a permit naming `RESP` and `ATND`, `direct`, the purpose `TREAT` and the confidentiality `V`). RBAC, ABAC and relationship-based control thus sit in one actor scope: `_role_` reads the `ROLE` a caller performs, `_organisation_type_` and `_caller_kind_` with a directive's `_purposes_` and `_data_` read attributes of caller, request and data, and `_relationship_`, `_care_roles_` and `_care_path_` read the relationships between caller and subject.

=== The Default and the Gate-keepers

`_default_access_` answers for an access no directive decides, in the `access default` group: `open`, `restricted` or `consent_required`. This is the "sensible default" of the Security Policy, set per EHR. `_gate_keepers_` names the parties who may commit the next version of the `EHR_ACCESS`: usually `PARTY_SELF`, otherwise a parent, guardian or other responsible party referenced into the demographic service (Security Policy, "Access control of access settings").

=== Directives

An `ACCESS_DIRECTIVE` is one instruction about access to the data of the EHR. `_kind_` says what it is (`consent`, `restriction`, `opt_out`, `delegation`, or `controller_rule` for a rule of the controller's policy) and `_effect_` whether it permits or denies. It applies to an access when every attribute it sets matches the access:

* `_applies_to_`: the callers, as `ACCESS_ACTOR_SCOPE` instances, any one of which is enough. A scope names a demographic party (a `PERSON` or `AGENT` matches itself; an `ORGANISATION` or `GROUP` matches whoever acts for it or is a member), a role, an organisation type, a relationship to the subject (`care`, `representative`, `proxy`) or a kind of caller. A role is matched against the coded `_name_` of each `ROLE` in force the caller performs, the type of the role (RM Demographic IM, `PARTY.type()`, invariant `type = name`), so the demographic service is the one source of who holds which role. An organisation type is matched the same way against the coded `_name_` of the `ORGANISATION` the caller acts for. A caller from outside the system, admitted under an agreement, is described by no party of the demographic service, so the professional role and the type of its healthcare facility its verified credential attests match `_role_` and `_organisation_type_` as the coded names would. A national rule, such as one that keeps insurers or occupational physicians out of an exchange, is a controller rule over these attributes with the provision in its `_legal_grounds_`; the decision procedure holds no rule of any jurisdiction.
* `_operations_` and `_purposes_`: the operations and the purposes of use, coded in the terminologies the system documents and accepts.
* `_data_`: the data, as `ACCESS_DATA_SCOPE` instances: single versioned objects, templates, archetypes (with their specialisations), categories, sensitivities, origins, the episodes of care the data are placed in, and the interval in which the data were committed. A restriction of one episode's data is a restriction of "parts" of the record (EHDS Art 8).
* `_time_validity_`: when the directive is in force, to the second.

An empty list applies to everything of its kind, so a directive with no `_data_` covers the whole EHR, including data recorded after it. A directive outside its `_time_validity_` is kept as history and decides nothing.

`_exceptions_` holds narrower directives of the opposite effect, one level deep, such as "deny mental health data, except to my GP". `_emergency_override_` states whether a declared emergency lifts the directive (EHDS Art 11(5) for a restriction; Art 10(2) for an opt-out where national law allows it). `_instructed_by_`, `_instructed_`, `_legal_grounds_`, `_evidence_` and `_identifiers_` record who gave the instruction, when, on which legal ground, where the signed record of it sits (the `ADMIN_ENTRY` of the consent, RM EHR IM Entry Package), and its identifiers in other systems. `_details_` carries the deployment's archetyped detail, which the procedure never reads.

The kinds carry the rights of the subject:

[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
|Kind |Use

|`consent`
|A permit or deny the subject gives: share with a named party or organisation, or refuse one.

|`restriction`
|A deny of professionals' access to all or part of the record (EHDS Art 8). The data read as absent, with no trace of the restriction.

|`opt_out`
|A deny of the whole record to the access services (EHDS Art 10). At most one per EHR.

|`delegation`
|The scope of what a proxy or representative may read (EHDS Art 4(2)), narrowing the relationship the demographic service records.

|`controller_rule`
|A rule of the controller's `ACCESS_POLICY` for every EHR it keeps (see <<_the_controllers_rules>>). Never a directive of an EHR, of the catalogue or of an exception of either.
|===

=== Labels and Classifications

`ACCESS_LABEL` puts sensitivity codes, a confidentiality marking, or both on one versioned object of the EHR: a safety assessment in a case of abuse can be `R` while the rest of the record stays `N`. `ACCESS_CLASSIFICATION`, held by the policy, gives every datum a scope describes the same codes and a confidentiality: "psychiatric notes are `PSY`". A classification names no single object. The sensitivity codes come from the terminology the jurisdiction names, such as the `HL7_ActCode` sensitivity policy codes, so the privacy levels are "defined by standards or agreements within jurisdictions of use" (Security Policy, Privacy); the confidentiality is always an `HL7_Confidentiality` code, whose order the package fixes.

=== The Controller Policy

`ACCESS_POLICY._controller_` names the `ORGANISATION` whose policy it is. `_care_relationship_required_` makes a professional's clinical access depend on a live care relationship to the subject, and `_relationship_grace_` extends the relationship after it ends for follow-up (Security Policy, "Time-limitation of access"). `_purpose_required_` makes a declared purpose of use mandatory. `_catalogue_` lists named directives a subject accepts by an identifier, such as an IHE BPPC policy or a national consent choice; accepting one copies its directive into the subject's settings, with the signed acknowledgement as `_evidence_`.

=== The Controller's Facts about Care Relationships

The policy states how the controller's care relationships are read and written:

Each default is the strictest reading, so a controller that sets nothing gets a system that admits least.

=== Profiles

The content these attributes take (value sets, periods, purposes) belongs to the deployment and its jurisdiction, and the package ships none. A deployment that wants to fix it for many controllers writes a *profile*: an openEHR template of the controller's `ACCESS_POLICY` archetype that fixes some attributes and leaves the controller the rest, with the value sets it binds held on a terminology server. A committed policy validates against its template as any archetyped data does. The profile adds no class, no step and no rule language. A fictional example, the profile of the imaginary state of Arcadia:

----
template  Arcadia hospital access policy   (specialises openEHR-EHR-ACCESS_POLICY.controller.v1)
  time_zone              = "Europe/Arcadia"        (fixed)
  care_purposes          = HL7_ActReason TREAT, ETREAT     (fixed)
  relationship_validity  = [ roles: any, default_period: P30D, max_period: P180D ]   (fixed)
  value_sets             = [ care_role -> http://terminology.arcadia.example/ValueSet/care-role ]   (fixed)
  rules                  = (left to the controller)
----


=== The Representation as a Stored Fact

EHDS Art 4(2) lets a natural person authorise others and leaves the acts of legal representatives to national law; GDPR Art 8(1), for information society services offered directly to a child, asks for consent "given or authorised by the holder of parental responsibility over the child". Who holds that responsibility, from which age a minor decides alone, and whether a person can decide for themselves are questions of national law and of the care delivery environment (xref:BASE:architecture_overview:security.adoc[Security], The Problem of Roles). The package therefore holds no age band, no capacity rule and no legal-basis table: the writer stores a `represents` relationship with its basis, its provider and its period, ends it when the representation ends, and the system decides a representative's request on that stored fact, as it decides a care relationship, and records which representation it rested on.

A per-service confidentiality that binds a representative, such as a service a minor lawfully consented to alone, is a fact of the object: a `restriction` of the subject whose `_applies_to_` names the representative relationship, or a label at `R` under a rule for representatives.

=== The Controller's Rules

EHDS Art 11(3) asks each Member State to lay down "the categories of personal electronic health data accessible by different categories of health professionals or for different healthcare tasks", and Art 11(4) applies the rules of the Member State of treatment. IHE APPC places the same kind of rule in a foundational privacy policy of the policy domain, which says "which functional roles may access which document, and under which conditions" (ITI TF-1 43.4.1), and FHIR R5 leaves role-based rules out of `Consent` (R5 `Consent` §6.2.3). `ACCESS_POLICY._rules_` holds these rules: one `ACCESS_DIRECTIVE` of kind `controller_rule` each, with the scopes, operations, purposes, validity, exceptions and legal grounds a directive carries.

A rule binds every caller of every EHR the controller keeps, apart from the subject reading or writing their own record, whose right of access (EHDS Art 3) is not the controller's to deny. A deny rule refuses what it covers whatever the subject's directives permit: who may record or read which kind of data is the competence of the care delivery environment (xref:BASE:architecture_overview:security.adoc[Security], The Problem of Roles), not the subject's to grant. A permit rule clears callers for the sensitivities it names, the way a subject's permit directive does, and decides nothing else. A rule with a permit exception states "only": "nurses may write no template except these", or "professionals may declare no purpose except treatment". Each rule's exceptions are rules too.

The rules name kinds of data best by the sensitivities `_classifications_` assign, so a template id appears in one place, the classification. A rule may name `_templates_` directly, as a rule about writing usually does: for a write, the data are the version being committed. `_evidence_` and `_instructed_by_` have no use on a rule; `_legal_grounds_` names the provision it rests on.

The rules, their place in the procedure, the reading of `_role_` from the demographic `ROLE` and of `_organisation_type_` from the demographic `ORGANISATION`, and the reading of both from an attested credential are this proposal's own; no openEHR specification has a controller rule.

=== Restrictions of the Access Log's Subject View

EHDS Art 9(2) gives the person access to who accessed their data, and Art 9(3) lets a Member State restrict that right "in exceptional circumstances". `ACCESS_POLICY._log_restrictions_` declares the restrictions the controller's law lays down. Each `ACCESS_LOG_RESTRICTION` names the kinds of accessor it applies to, in the `accessor kind` group the Access Log Information Model codes `ACCESSOR._kind_` in, the attributes of `ACCESSOR` the subject view withholds, in the `access log withheld` group (xref:terminology.adoc[Terminology]), and the provisions that lay it down, in the terminology the jurisdiction names. A system serves the subject view of an entry whose accessor is of a named kind to the subject and their representative without those attributes; the entry itself is unchanged, and a reviewer reads it whole. A change to the restrictions changes what the log means, so a system records it in the log. The package carries no country's restriction.

=== Retention of the Access Log

EHDS Art 9(2) requires the access information to "be available for at least three years from each date of access to the data", and national law may set a longer minimum or a maximum. Which law binds the controller, and how long within it the log keeps an entry, is the controller's decision: GDPR Art 5(1)(e) makes the period of storage the controller's to set. `ACCESS_POLICY._log_retention_` states it. `ACCESS_LOG_RETENTION._in_union_` declares whether Regulation (EU) 2025/327 binds the controller, `_period_` how long each entry is kept from the date of access, and `_floors_` and `_ceilings_` the minimum and maximum periods the controller's law sets, each an `ACCESS_RETENTION_BOUND` with the provision that sets it, coded in the terminology the jurisdiction names. The invariants refuse a policy whose period breaks a bound it declares, a period under the three years of Art 9(2) when `_in_union_` holds, a ceiling below a floor, and a ceiling with no period, since an entry kept without end never stays within one.

A system writes each access-log entry with the period and grounds of the policy in force (Access Log Information Model, `ACCESS_RETENTION`): Art 9(2) when `_in_union_` holds, then each declared floor and ceiling, and the version of the policy they came from. With no `_log_retention_` the system keeps each entry without end, which meets every floor, and says the period is undecided. The package carries no country's bounds.
