= The access_control Package

include::partial$draft_notice.adoc[]

== Overview

An `ACCESS_DIRECTIVE_SETTINGS` is the `_settings_` of one `EHR_ACCESS`, and `EHR_ACCESS.scheme()` returns `access_directives` for it. It holds the default disposition of the EHR, the gate-keepers, the `ACCESS_DIRECTIVE` instances the subject gave and the `ACCESS_LABEL` instances on single objects. An `ACCESS_POLICY`, kept in a `VERSIONED_ACCESS_POLICY`, holds the controller's facts, its own rules for every EHR and the restrictions of the access log's subject view among them. `ACCESS_ACTOR_SCOPE` and `ACCESS_DATA_SCOPE` describe callers and data, and are used by directives, labels and classifications alike.

== Design

=== The Default and the Gate-keepers

`_default_access_` answers for an access no directive decides, in the `access default` group: `open`, `restricted` or `consent_required`. This is the "sensible default" of the Security Policy, set per EHR. `_gate_keepers_` names the parties who may commit the next version of the `EHR_ACCESS`: usually `PARTY_SELF`, otherwise a parent, guardian or other responsible party referenced into the demographic service (Security Policy, "Access control of access settings").

=== Directives

An `ACCESS_DIRECTIVE` is one instruction about access to the data of the EHR. `_kind_` says what it is (`consent`, `restriction`, `opt_out`, `delegation`, or `controller_rule` for a rule of the controller's policy) and `_effect_` whether it permits or denies. It applies to an access when every attribute it sets matches the access:

* `_applies_to_`: the callers, as `ACCESS_ACTOR_SCOPE` instances, any one of which is enough. A scope names a demographic party (a `PERSON` or `AGENT` matches itself; an `ORGANISATION` or `GROUP` matches whoever acts for it or is a member), a role, an organisation type, a relationship to the subject (`care`, `representative`, `proxy`) or a kind of caller. A role is matched against the coded `_name_` of each `ROLE` in force the caller performs, the type of the role (RM Demographic IM, `PARTY.type()`, invariant `type = name`), so the demographic service is the one source of who holds which role. An organisation type is matched the same way against the coded `_name_` of the `ORGANISATION` the caller acts for. A caller from outside the system, admitted under an agreement, is described by no party of the demographic service, so the professional role and the type of its healthcare facility its verified credential attests match `_role_` and `_organisation_type_` as the coded names would. A national rule, such as one that keeps insurers or occupational physicians out of an exchange, is a controller rule over these attributes with the provision in its `_legal_grounds_`; the decision procedure holds no rule of any jurisdiction.
* `_operations_` and `_purposes_`: the operations and the purposes of use, coded in the terminologies the system documents and accepts.
* `_data_`: the data, as `ACCESS_DATA_SCOPE` instances: single versioned objects, templates, archetypes (with their specialisations), categories, sensitivities, origins, and the interval in which the data were committed.
* `_time_validity_`: when the directive is in force, to the second.

An empty list applies to everything of its kind, so a directive with no `_data_` covers the whole EHR, including data recorded after it. A directive outside its `_time_validity_` is kept as history and decides nothing.

`_exceptions_` holds narrower directives of the opposite effect, one level deep, such as "deny mental health data, except to my GP". `_emergency_override_` states whether a declared emergency lifts the directive (EHDS Art 11(5) for a restriction; Art 10(2) for an opt-out where national law allows it). `_instructed_by_`, `_instructed_`, `_legal_grounds_`, `_evidence_` and `_identifiers_` record who gave the instruction, when, on which legal ground, where the signed record of it sits (the `ADMIN_ENTRY` of the consent, RM EHR IM Entry Package), and its identifiers in other systems. `_details_` carries the deployment's archetyped detail, which the procedure never reads.

The kinds carry the rights of the subject:

[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
|Kind |Use

|`consent`
|A permit or deny the subject gives: share with a named party or organisation, or refuse one.

|`restriction`
|A deny of professionals' access to all or part of the record (EHDS Art 8). The data read as absent, with no trace of the restriction.

|`opt_out`
|A deny of the whole record to the access services (EHDS Art 10). At most one per EHR.

|`delegation`
|The scope of what a proxy or representative may read (EHDS Art 4(2)), narrowing the relationship the demographic service records.

|`controller_rule`
|A rule of the controller's `ACCESS_POLICY` for every EHR it keeps (see <<_the_controllers_rules>>). Never a directive of an EHR, of the catalogue or of an exception of either.
|===

=== Labels and Classifications

`ACCESS_LABEL` puts sensitivity codes on one versioned object of the EHR. `ACCESS_CLASSIFICATION`, held by the policy, gives every datum a scope describes the same codes: "psychiatric notes are `PSY`". A classification names no single object. The codes come from the terminology the jurisdiction names, such as the HL7 v3 confidentiality and sensitivity codes, so the privacy levels are "defined by standards or agreements within jurisdictions of use" (Security Policy, Privacy).

=== The Controller Policy

`ACCESS_POLICY._controller_` names the `ORGANISATION` whose policy it is. `_care_relationship_required_` makes a professional's clinical access depend on a live care relationship to the subject, and `_relationship_grace_` extends the relationship after it ends for follow-up (Security Policy, "Time-limitation of access"). `_purpose_required_` makes a declared purpose of use mandatory. `_catalogue_` lists named directives a subject accepts by an identifier, such as an IHE BPPC policy or a national consent choice; accepting one copies its directive into the subject's settings, with the signed acknowledgement as `_evidence_`.

=== The Controller's Rules

EHDS Art 11(3) asks each Member State to lay down "the categories of personal electronic health data accessible by different categories of health professionals or for different healthcare tasks", and Art 11(4) applies the rules of the Member State of treatment. IHE APPC places the same kind of rule in a foundational privacy policy of the policy domain, which says "which functional roles may access which document, and under which conditions" (ITI TF-1 43.4.1), and FHIR R5 leaves role-based rules out of `Consent` (R5 `Consent` §6.2.3). `ACCESS_POLICY._rules_` holds these rules: one `ACCESS_DIRECTIVE` of kind `controller_rule` each, with the scopes, operations, purposes, validity, exceptions and legal grounds a directive carries.

A rule binds every caller of every EHR the controller keeps, apart from the subject reading or writing their own record, whose right of access (EHDS Art 3) is not the controller's to deny. A deny rule refuses what it covers whatever the subject's directives permit: who may record or read which kind of data is the competence of the care delivery environment (xref:BASE:architecture_overview:security.adoc[Security], The Problem of Roles), not the subject's to grant. A permit rule clears callers for the sensitivities it names, the way a subject's permit directive does, and decides nothing else. A rule with a permit exception states "only": "nurses may write no template except these", or "professionals may declare no purpose except treatment". Each rule's exceptions are rules too.

The rules name kinds of data best by the sensitivities `_classifications_` assign, so a template id appears in one place, the classification. A rule may name `_templates_` directly, as a rule about writing usually does: for a write, the data are the version being committed. `_evidence_` and `_instructed_by_` have no use on a rule; `_legal_grounds_` names the provision it rests on.

The rules, their place in the procedure, the reading of `_role_` from the demographic `ROLE` and of `_organisation_type_` from the demographic `ORGANISATION`, and the reading of both from an attested credential are this proposal's own; no openEHR specification has a controller rule.

=== Restrictions of the Access Log's Subject View

EHDS Art 9(2) gives the person access to who accessed their data, and Art 9(3) lets a Member State restrict that right "in exceptional circumstances". `ACCESS_POLICY._log_restrictions_` declares the restrictions the controller's law lays down. Each `ACCESS_LOG_RESTRICTION` names the kinds of accessor it applies to, in the `accessor kind` group the Access Log Information Model codes `ACCESSOR._kind_` in, the attributes of `ACCESSOR` the subject view withholds, in the `access log withheld` group (xref:terminology.adoc[Terminology]), and the provisions that lay it down, in the terminology the jurisdiction names. A system serves the subject view of an entry whose accessor is of a named kind to the subject and their representative without those attributes; the entry itself is unchanged, and a reviewer reads it whole. A change to the restrictions changes what the log means, so a system records it in the log. The package carries no country's restriction.

=== Retention of the Access Log

EHDS Art 9(2) requires the access information to "be available for at least three years from each date of access to the data", and national law may set a longer minimum or a maximum. Which law binds the controller, and how long within it the log keeps an entry, is the controller's decision: GDPR Art 5(1)(e) makes the period of storage the controller's to set. `ACCESS_POLICY._log_retention_` states it. `ACCESS_LOG_RETENTION._in_union_` declares whether Regulation (EU) 2025/327 binds the controller, `_period_` how long each entry is kept from the date of access, and `_floors_` and `_ceilings_` the minimum and maximum periods the controller's law sets, each an `ACCESS_RETENTION_BOUND` with the provision that sets it, coded in the terminology the jurisdiction names. The invariants refuse a policy whose period breaks a bound it declares, a period under the three years of Art 9(2) when `_in_union_` holds, a ceiling below a floor, and a ceiling with no period, since an entry kept without end never stays within one.

A system writes each access-log entry with the period and grounds of the policy in force (Access Log Information Model, `ACCESS_RETENTION`): Art 9(2) when `_in_union_` holds, then each declared floor and ceiling, and the version of the policy they came from. With no `_log_retention_` the system keeps each entry without end, which meets every floor, and says the period is undecided. The package carries no country's bounds.
