= The Decision Procedure

include::partial$draft_notice.adoc[]

== Overview

The scheme `access_directives` fixes how an access is decided from the facts of the three sets (xref:design_overview.adoc#_three_sets_of_facts[Three Sets of Facts]): the subject's directives, the controller's policy with its rules, and the caller's facts in the demographic service. No openEHR or other specification gives such a procedure; this one is the proposal's own, the place of the controller's rules in it included. A system MUST decide every access to an EHR whose `EHR_ACCESS.scheme()` is `access_directives` by this procedure, after it has authenticated the caller and checked that the caller's capabilities grant the operation at all. The procedure adds conditions to that check and never grants an operation the caller's capabilities do not grant.

== Terms

* A directive is *in force* at the time of the access when the access falls within its `_time_validity_`, or the attribute is void.
* A directive *applies* to an access when it is in force, every attribute it sets matches the access, and none of its `_exceptions_` applies. An exception applies when every attribute it sets matches the access. A rule of `ACCESS_POLICY._rules_` applies on the same terms.
* A caller *performs a role* when it is the `_performer_` of a `ROLE` in force (its `_time_validity_` holds the date of the access, and its versioned object is not deleted) whose `_name_` is a `DV_CODED_TEXT` of that role's terminology and code (RM Demographic IM, `PARTY.type()`). An actor scope's `_role_` matches a caller that performs it.
* The *data* of a read are the stored objects it reaches. The data of a write are the version being committed: its template (`archetype_details.template_id`), its root archetype, its origins and the sensitivities the policy's classifications give it.
* A caller *declares an emergency* when it states a purpose of use the system documents as an emergency purpose and holds the role the system's emergency rules require.
* The *labels* of a datum are the sensitivities of the `ACCESS_LABEL` instances that target it and of the `ACCESS_POLICY` classifications whose scope it falls in.
* A caller has a *live care relationship* to the subject when it is a member of a care-team `GROUP` (or acts for an organisational unit) that the subject's `PERSON` is under the care of, within the validity of both relationships plus the policy's `_relationship_grace_`.

== Procedure

The steps run in order for each EHR the access reaches. The first step that refuses decides.

[cols="1,5,2", options="header"]
|===
|Step |Rule |Refusal

|0. Subject
|A caller of kind `subject` is the subject of the one EHR its authenticated context names, and a caller whose grants reach only the EHR of its authenticated context reaches that EHR alone. Every other EHR, and an operation on EHR data that names no EHR, MUST be refused to either, whatever any EHR's directives permit. On its own EHR, a caller of another kind takes every further step.
|`refused-not-subject`

|0. Representation
|A caller of kind `representative` acts for the subject a recorded representation `PARTY_RELATIONSHIP` names, and reaches the one EHR of that subject alone. Every other EHR, and an operation on EHR data that names no EHR, MUST be refused to it, whatever any EHR's directives permit. On that EHR it takes steps 1 to 4, the `delegation` directives of its `EHR_ACCESS` included.
|`refused-not-represented`

|1. Opt-out
|An `opt_out` directive in force binds callers of kind `access_service`. Unless its `_emergency_override_` holds and the caller declares an emergency, and the caller does not act for the subject, the EHR MUST be answered as one that does not exist.
|`refused-opt-out`, not shown

|2. Restriction
|A `restriction` directive that applies binds callers of kind `professional` and the systems that act for a provider. Data it covers MUST be answered as data that do not exist: a named object as absent, and left out of queries and exports. A caller that declares an emergency, where `_emergency_override_` holds, passes, and the access MUST be marked as an emergency override (EHDS Art 11(5)).
|`refused-restricted`, not shown

|3. Consent deny
|Any other deny directive that applies refuses.
|`refused-consent-denied`

|4. Controller rule
|A deny rule of `ACCESS_POLICY._rules_` that applies refuses, whatever the subject's directives permit, unless its `_emergency_override_` holds and the caller declares an emergency.
|`refused-policy-denied`

|5. Sensitivity
|A datum with a label that no permit directive that applies and no permit rule that applies names is refused when named and left out of a query.
|`refused-sensitivity`

|6. Care relationship
|Where `ACCESS_POLICY._care_relationship_required_` holds and the caller is a professional, the caller needs a live care relationship to the subject, a permit directive that applies, or an entry in `_gate_keepers_`.
|`refused-no-care-relationship`

|7. Purpose
|Where `ACCESS_POLICY._purpose_required_` holds, an access without a declared purpose is refused. Which purposes a caller may declare is a rule of step 4: a deny rule with a permit exception naming the purposes.
|`refused-purpose-missing`

|8. Default
|`open` admits. `restricted` admits a caller that a permit directive that applies, an entry in `_gate_keepers_` or a live care relationship names. `consent_required` admits only a caller that a permit directive that applies names.
|`refused-consent-required`
|===

Directives and rules combine by deny-overrides: where a permit and a deny both apply, the deny decides. A permit directive decides only through the steps that ask for one (5, 6 and 8). A permit rule decides only through step 5, and a rule's permit exception only by narrowing the deny rule it sits in. Classifications combine by union.

Step 4 comes after the subject's opt-out and restriction, so restricted data still read as absent rather than forbidden (EHDS Art 8, Art 11(5)). A controller deny is shown, because it reveals a rule of the controller and nothing about the subject. A permit of the subject cannot lift it: who may record or read which kind of data is the competence of the care delivery environment (xref:BASE:architecture_overview:security.adoc[Security], The Problem of Roles), not the subject's to grant. A permit rule acts only on sensitivity, where a permit of the subject also acts, so opening confidential data stays the subject's to do as well as the controller's.

The subject reading or writing their own record skips steps 3 to 8: the subject's own right of access (EHDS Art 3) is not the controller's to deny. A representative is held to steps 1 to 4: to the subject's directives, to any `delegation` directive that names them, and to the controller's rules, so a controller can keep, for example, a minor's confidential data from a parent. An EHR whose `EHR_ACCESS` has no settings is decided by the system's documented default.

A write is decided by steps 1 and 2 for the EHR as a whole and by steps 3 to 8 for the version it commits. A restriction and an opt-out limit access to data that exist, and writing new data is no access to them, so no write is refused for its content by either, and no write reveals one. Where the decision for a request depends on the datum, the first step that refuses that datum decides: no step about the EHR alone decides before a step about the datum. Both are this proposal's own.

== Refusals

A refusal that is shown is answered as forbidden, and the answer MAY carry the refusal. The refusals of steps 1 and 2 MUST NOT be shown: the fact that a person has restricted access "shall not be visible to healthcare providers" (EHDS Art 8). Every refusal, shown or not, SHOULD be recorded with its `access refusal` code (xref:terminology.adoc[Terminology]) in the system's access log, together with the directive, the `EHR_ACCESS` version and the `ACCESS_POLICY` version it rested on.

== Queries

A query MUST apply the same procedure to every row it could return, so that a count, a limit and a page cover only the rows the caller may read. A system that runs a query as one database statement applies the procedure as a condition of that statement.

== Reading and Writing the Settings

. A new version of an `EHR_ACCESS` whose settings are `ACCESS_DIRECTIVE_SETTINGS` MUST be committed only by a party its current `_gate_keepers_` name, by the subject, or by the controller acting on the subject's instruction.
. The settings MUST be read only by the same parties. To everyone else the `EHR_ACCESS` is answered as one that does not exist, because a restriction must not be visible to providers (EHDS Art 8).
. A system MUST check every new version against the invariants of the classes and the terminology groups of xref:terminology.adoc[Terminology] before it stores it, and refuse it whole when a check fails.
. A directive is withdrawn by a later version without it, so the history of the subject's choices is the version history of the `EHR_ACCESS` (Security Policy, "Audit trailing").

== Exchange

An EHR Extract carries the `EHR_ACCESS` versions in `X_VERSIONED_EHR_ACCESS`, so the subject's directives move with the record. A receiving system that implements the scheme applies them as they are. A system that does not sees a `scheme()` it does not know, and keeps the object as data. `ACCESS_POLICY` does not travel with an EHR: it belongs to the controller, not to the subject.
