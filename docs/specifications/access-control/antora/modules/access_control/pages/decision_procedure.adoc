= The Decision Procedure

include::partial$draft_notice.adoc[]

== Overview

The schemes `confidentiality` and `access_directives` fix how an access is decided from the facts of the three sets (xref:design_overview.adoc#_three_sets_of_facts[Three Sets of Facts]): the subject's directives and the controller's facts about the EHR, the controller's policy with its rules, and the caller's and the subject's facts in the relationship store. No openEHR or other specification gives such a procedure; this one is the proposal's own, the place of the controller's rules, the care relationship and the confidentiality in it included. A system MUST decide every access to an EHR whose `EHR_ACCESS.scheme()` is `confidentiality` or `access_directives` by this procedure, after it has authenticated the caller and checked that the caller's capabilities grant the operation at all. The procedure adds conditions to that check and never grants an operation the caller's capabilities do not grant. An EHR whose settings are a `CONFIDENTIALITY_SETTINGS` and no subtype is decided as one with no directive, no label and the `_default_access_` the system documents.

== Terms

* A directive is *in force* at the time of the access when the access falls within its `_time_validity_`, or the attribute is void.
* A directive *applies* to an access when it is in force, every attribute it sets matches the access, and none of its `_exceptions_` applies. An exception applies when every attribute it sets matches the access. A rule of `ACCESS_POLICY._rules_` and an exclusion of `CONFIDENTIALITY_SETTINGS._exclusions_` apply on the same terms.
* A caller *performs a role* when it is the `_performer_` of a `ROLE` in force (its `_time_validity_` holds the date of the access, and its versioned object is not deleted) whose `_name_` is a `DV_CODED_TEXT` of that role's terminology and code (RM Demographic IM, `PARTY.type()`). An actor scope's `_role_` matches a caller that performs it.
* The *data* of a read are the stored objects it reaches. The data of a write are the version being committed: its template (`archetype_details.template_id`), its root archetype, its origins, the sensitivities the policy's classifications give it and the confidentiality of its EHR.
* A caller *declares an emergency* when it states a purpose of use the system documents as an emergency purpose (where the policy binds a value set for `emergency_purpose`, a code of it) and holds the role the system's emergency rules require.
* The *labels* of a datum are the sensitivities of the `ACCESS_LABEL` instances that target it and of the `ACCESS_POLICY` classifications whose scope it falls in.
* The *confidentiality* of a datum is the highest `level()` of the marking of its EHR (`CONFIDENTIALITY_SETTINGS._marking_`), the marking of an `ACCESS_LABEL` that targets it, and the `_confidentiality_` of each classification whose scope it falls in. With none of them it is `N`.
* The *day* of an access is its date in the time zone of `ACCESS_POLICY._time_zone_`, or in UTC when that is void. A relationship's `_time_validity_` holds a day when the day is not before its lower bound and not after its upper bound; both bounds are included.
* A caller has a *live care relationship* to the subject when it holds one by xref:#_holding_a_care_relationship[Holding a Care Relationship], while it acts for the relationship's `provider` or one of its units, and the access declares no purpose or declares one of `ACCESS_POLICY._care_purposes_`.
* A caller is *in conflict* with the subject when the caller's own `PERSON` is the subject's `PERSON` (`self`), or a `related_to` relationship in force joins the two (`related`).
* The *subject's `PERSON`* is the `PERSON` the `EHR_STATUS._subject_._external_ref_` names: a reference in the namespace the system names its own parties under names that `PERSON` directly, and any other reference, such as a pseudonym an identity source issued, names the `PERSON` whose `_details_` carry an `ELEMENT` named by the `record subject` code whose `DV_IDENTIFIER` value has that reference's namespace as `_issuer_` and its id as `_id_` (Demographic IM §2.1.3). A reference no `PERSON` declares names none, and every relationship read for the subject's `PERSON` is then absent. A system without a demographic service addresses the same relationships by the subject reference itself (xref:access_control_package.adoc#_the_relationship_store[The Relationship Store]).

== Holding a Care Relationship

A caller *holds* a care relationship R (an `under_care_of` relationship whose `source` is the subject) on the day of the access by one of these paths, and by no other:

[cols="1,4,1", options="header"]
|===
|Path |Condition |Default

|`direct`
|R's `target` names the caller's `PERSON`, or a `ROLE` the caller performs, and R's `basis` is `assigned`.
|on

|`team`
|R's `target` names a `GROUP` or an `ORGANISATION` the caller is a member of by an `HL7_RoleClass::MBR` relationship in force, or a group or unit that such a group or unit is itself a member of: one level of nesting and no further.
|on

|`self_declared`
|R's `target` names the caller's `PERSON` and R's `basis` is `self_declared`, written by the caller within the period `ACCESS_POLICY._self_declared_care_` allows.
|off

|`attested`
|R is no stored relationship: the caller's verified credential, issued under an agreement `ACCESS_POLICY._attested_care_` names, attests a treatment context for this subject (the subject's identifier and a purpose of `_care_purposes_`) that is current on the day of the access. The role is `PART` unless the credential attests one of the `care_role` value set.
|off
|===

R is *live* on the day of the access when its `_time_validity_` holds the day, or the day is within `ACCESS_POLICY._relationship_grace_` after its end. A membership is live only within its own `_time_validity_`; grace never extends a membership. A path that is off matches no caller.

The resolution set is closed. A system MUST NOT follow a relationship through any other path, through more than one level of nesting, or from one professional to another: cover is a relationship of its own (`covers_for`), written for the period of the cover. A fixed set keeps the check a bounded lookup and makes two systems agree on who holds what. A relationship written without a role is not valid. No role implies another, and a path implies no other: a rule that names `direct` is not met by a `self_declared` or an `attested` relationship.

An actor scope with `_relationship_` `care` applies to a caller that holds a live care relationship whose `care_role` is one of `_care_roles_` (when set) by the path `_care_path_` names (when set; when void, `direct` or `team`, and `self_declared` or `attested` where the policy turns that path on). Where several relationships admit an access, the one the access log names is chosen in this order: `direct` before `team` before `self_declared` before `attested`, then by the order of the roles in the scope that matched.

== Procedure

The steps run in order for each EHR the access reaches. The first step that refuses decides.

[cols="1,5,2", options="header"]
|===
|Step |Rule |Refusal

|0. Subject
|A caller of kind `datasubject` (`HL7_SecurityRoleType`) is the subject of the one EHR its authenticated context names, and a caller whose grants reach only the EHR of its authenticated context reaches that EHR alone. Every other EHR, and an operation on EHR data that names no EHR, MUST be refused to either, whatever any EHR's directives permit. On its own EHR, a caller of another kind takes every further step.
|`refused-not-subject`

|0. Representation
|A caller of kind `representative` acts for the subject a recorded `represents` relationship names, and reaches the one EHR of that subject alone, while the representation is live on the day of the access and the caller acts for its `provider`. Every other EHR, and an operation on EHR data that names no EHR, MUST be refused to it, whatever any EHR's directives permit. The system decides on the stored representation and never derives one from the subject's age or capacity: the writer decides who represents whom (xref:BASE:architecture_overview:security.adoc[Security], The Problem of Roles). On that EHR it takes the exclusion and steps 1 to 3 and 5, the `delegation` directives of its `EHR_ACCESS` included.
|`refused-not-represented`

|0. Consent reference
|A caller admitted under an agreement that conditions its reads on a consent decision, whose credential carries no reference to that decision, MUST be refused every read, query and export, whatever any EHR's directives permit. The rule judges the caller's agreement, never the EHR.
|`refused-consent-reference-missing`

|0. Exclusion
|An exclusion of `CONFIDENTIALITY_SETTINGS._exclusions_` that applies refuses the caller, a representative and a gate-keeper included. A permit of the subject or of a representative does not lift it, and it never binds the subject. An emergency lifts it only where its `_emergency_override_` holds and the caller is not a representative.
|`refused-excluded`

|1. Opt-out
|An `opt_out` directive in force binds callers of kind `access_service`. Unless its `_emergency_override_` holds and the caller declares an emergency, and the caller does not act for the subject, the EHR MUST be answered as one that does not exist.
|`refused-opt-out`, not shown

|2. Restriction
|A `restriction` directive that applies binds callers of kind `professional`, the systems that act for a provider, and a caller of kind `representative` its `_applies_to_` names by party or by the `representative` relationship. Data it covers MUST be answered as data that do not exist: a named object as absent, and left out of queries and exports. A caller that declares an emergency, where `_emergency_override_` holds, passes, and the access MUST be marked as an emergency override (EHDS Art 11(5)).
|`refused-restricted`, not shown

|3. Consent deny
|Any other deny directive that applies refuses.
|`refused-consent-denied`

|4. Purpose
|Where `ACCESS_POLICY._purpose_required_` holds, an access without a declared purpose is refused, whatever rules the policy holds. The check reads the request alone and runs before any rule is read. Which purposes a caller may declare is a rule of step 5: a deny rule with a permit exception naming the purposes.
|`refused-purpose-missing`

|5. Conflict
|A professional caller in conflict with the subject is refused the professional path, unless an `ACCESS_POLICY._conflict_rules_` entry of that conflict names the caller (and, for `related`, the kinship). An access admitted under such an entry MUST be marked in the access log. The subject reaches their own record through the subject path, as step 0 allows.
|`refused-conflict`

|5. Controller rule
|A deny rule of `ACCESS_POLICY._rules_` that applies refuses, whatever the subject's directives permit, unless its `_emergency_override_` holds and the caller declares an emergency. A rule that sets `_purposes_` applies only to an access that declares one of them.
|`refused-policy-denied`

|6. Sensitivity
|A datum with a label that no permit directive that applies and no permit rule that applies names is refused when named and left out of a query.
|`refused-sensitivity`

|6. Confidentiality
|A datum whose confidentiality is above `N` that no permit directive that applies and no permit rule that applies clears is refused when named and left out of a query. A permit clears a datum when one of its `_data_` scopes names, in `_confidentialities_`, the datum's level or a higher one.
|`refused-confidentiality`

|7. Care relationship
|Where `ACCESS_POLICY._care_relationship_required_` holds and the caller is a professional, the caller needs a live care relationship to the subject, a permit directive that applies, or an entry in `_gate_keepers_`.
|`refused-no-care-relationship`

|8. Default
|`open` admits. `restricted` admits a caller that a permit directive that applies, an entry in `_gate_keepers_` or a live care relationship names. `consent_required` admits only a caller that a permit directive that applies names.
|`refused-consent-required`
|===

A purpose is the one fact of an access the caller declares; the system establishes every other (the roles a caller performs, the type of the organisation it acts for, its care relationships and their path), and a value of a directive it cannot read is read against the caller: a deny it narrows covers the access, and a permit it narrows does not. A missing purpose is controlled by `_purpose_required_` at step 4, so a rule's purpose scope matches only a purpose the access declares, and a rule about one purpose, such as "no medical examination", refuses that purpose and nothing more. A directive of the subject keeps the reading of the other facts for its purposes: the subject has no `_purpose_required_` to set, so an access that declares no purpose stays under a deny the subject gave for a purpose. A care relationship is a ground for primary use only: an access that declares a purpose outside `_care_purposes_` is admitted by no relationship at steps 7 and 8 and matches no actor scope whose `_relationship_` is `care`. These readings are this proposal's own.

Directives and rules combine by deny-overrides: where a permit and a deny both apply, the deny decides. A permit directive decides only through the steps that ask for one (6, 7 and 8). A permit rule decides only through step 6, and a rule's permit exception only by narrowing the deny rule it sits in. Classifications combine by union.

Steps 4 and 5 come after the subject's opt-out and restriction, so restricted data still read as absent rather than forbidden (EHDS Art 8, Art 11(5)). A controller deny is shown, because it reveals a rule of the controller and nothing about the subject. A permit of the subject cannot lift it: who may record or read which kind of data is the competence of the care delivery environment (xref:BASE:architecture_overview:security.adoc[Security], The Problem of Roles), not the subject's to grant. A permit rule acts only on sensitivity and confidentiality, where a permit of the subject also acts, so opening confidential data stays the subject's to do as well as the controller's.

With no rule that names a confidentiality, an EHR marked `R` or `V` is read by its subject alone: the safe state needs no configuration. A care relationship is a ground in steps 7 and 8 and, through a rule's actor scope, in steps 5 and 6; it never lifts an exclusion, the subject's opt-out, restriction or consent deny, or a conflict. An emergency lifts steps 6 to 8 and each rule or directive whose `_emergency_override_` holds, and creates no relationship.

The subject reading or writing their own record skips steps 3 to 8: the subject's own right of access (EHDS Art 3) is not the controller's to deny. A representative is held to the exclusion and to steps 1 to 3 and 5: to the subject's directives, to any `delegation` directive that names them, and to the controller's rules, so a controller can keep, for example, a minor's confidential data from a parent. An EHR whose `EHR_ACCESS` has no settings is decided by the system's documented default.

A write is decided by steps 1 and 2 for the EHR as a whole and by the other steps for the version it commits. A restriction and an opt-out limit access to data that exist, and writing new data is no access to them, so no write is refused for its content by either, and no write reveals one. Where the decision for a request depends on the datum, the first step that refuses that datum decides: no step about the EHR alone decides before a step about the datum. Both are this proposal's own.

The worked example of the appendix (xref:appendix.adoc#_who_sees_what_informative[Who Sees What]) shows the procedure on one patient's care relationships at the three levels.

== Refusals

A refusal that is shown is answered as forbidden, and the answer MAY carry the refusal. The refusals of steps 1 and 2 MUST NOT be shown: the fact that a person has restricted access "shall not be visible to healthcare providers" (EHDS Art 8). A shown `refused-excluded` MUST NOT carry the exclusion or its reason. A shown `refused-confidentiality` MUST NOT carry the level or the marking's reason, so `R` and `V` answer alike; a named record whose confidentiality is one of `ACCESS_POLICY._concealed_confidentialities_` is answered as one that does not exist instead. A query, an export and a look-up of an EHR by its subject always leave a refused record out. Every refusal, shown or not, SHOULD be recorded with its `access refusal` code (xref:terminology.adoc[Terminology]) in the system's access log, together with the directive, the `EHR_ACCESS` version and the `ACCESS_POLICY` version it rested on, and, for `refused-confidentiality`, the level.

An access admitted on a care relationship SHOULD be recorded with the relationship it rested on: its role, its path (with the group or unit for `team`, and the assertion and agreement for `attested`), its uid and version, whether grace admitted it, its `covers_for` and its `provider`. A query records this for each EHR that contributed a row.

== Queries

A query MUST apply the same procedure to every row it could return, so that a count, a limit and a page cover only the rows the caller may read. A system that runs a query as one database statement applies the procedure as a condition of that statement. A query MAY be narrowed to the caller's caseload (xref:its_rest.adoc#_queries_and_the_caseload[Queries and the Caseload]): the EHRs whose subjects the caller holds a live care relationship to, by the same liveness and the same paths, applied before the result is counted, limited or paged. The caseload only narrows; the procedure still decides every row.

== Reading and Writing the Settings

. A new version of an `EHR_ACCESS` whose settings are `ACCESS_DIRECTIVE_SETTINGS` MUST be committed only by a party its current `_gate_keepers_` name, by the subject while the current `_gate_keepers_` is empty, by the controller acting on the subject's instruction, or by the controller for the attributes of `CONFIDENTIALITY_SETTINGS`. A competent subject is their own gate-keeper until one is named; `_gate_keepers_` that do not include `PARTY_SELF` take the subject's write away, and changing `_gate_keepers_` is itself such a write.
. `_marking_` and `_exclusions_` MUST be changed only by the controller. A version committed by anyone else MUST carry them unchanged, and a version that changes them otherwise is refused whole.
. The settings MUST be read only by the parties that may commit them. To everyone else the `EHR_ACCESS` is answered as one that does not exist, because a restriction must not be visible to providers (EHDS Art 8). The subject reads the marking's level, `_set_at_`, `_reason_` and the organisation that set it, never `_exclusions_`; a representative and a gate-keeper other than the subject read the settings without the marking and the exclusions.
. A system MUST check every new version against the invariants of the classes and the terminology groups of xref:terminology.adoc[Terminology] before it stores it, and refuse it whole when a check fails.
. A directive is withdrawn by a later version without it, so the history of the subject's choices is the version history of the `EHR_ACCESS` (Security Policy, "Audit trailing").
. A change takes effect for the next decision. A system MUST NOT decide on a care relationship, a membership, a marking or an exclusion it has not confirmed to be current for that decision.

== Writing Relationships

. A care relationship, a membership, a representation and a subject fact MUST be written only by a party the controller has authorised for that kind of relationship, for the providers and the kinds of target its authorisation names: the care delivery environment's systems (admission, rostering, the practice system) or an administrator. A write outside the writer's providers or target kinds is refused. The subject does not write care relationships; the subject's instrument is a directive in `EHR_ACCESS`, and a subject who grants a named professional access does so with a `consent` permit directive.
. A care relationship to a subject whose EHR is marked above `N` MUST be written only by a writer `ACCESS_POLICY._relationship_writers_` names for that level, and each such write SHOULD be recorded in the access log for review.
. An `ORGANISATION` is a target only when `ACCESS_POLICY._team_targets_` names it; the controller itself is never a target.
. A caller MUST NOT be admitted on a relationship it wrote naming itself, unless the relationship's `basis` is `self_declared` and `ACCESS_POLICY._self_declared_care_` allows it; a `self_declared` relationship is refused at write otherwise, and one longer than that period is refused.
. A care relationship written without an end takes the `_default_period_` of the first `ACCESS_POLICY._relationship_validity_` entry that covers its role and provider type; one longer than that entry's `_max_period_` is refused, and one no entry covers MUST be written with an end. An entry with neither period lets a relationship stay open.
. The writer ends a relationship by closing its `_time_validity_`, also when the subject dies; the system never ends one on its own judgement.

== Exchange

An EHR Extract carries the `EHR_ACCESS` versions in `X_VERSIONED_EHR_ACCESS`, so the subject's directives and the controller's marking move with the record. A receiving system that implements the scheme applies them as they are; a receiving controller that does not keep the sending controller's exclusions sets its own. A system that does not implement the scheme sees a `scheme()` it does not know, and keeps the object as data. `ACCESS_POLICY` does not travel with an EHR: it belongs to the controller, not to the subject. Care relationships do not travel with an EHR either: each is a fact of the provider in whose care it is.
