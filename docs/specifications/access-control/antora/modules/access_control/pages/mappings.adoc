== FHIR Consent

FHIR R4 and R5 both describe a `Consent` as "a base policy ... followed by a listing of exceptions to that policy" (R4 and R5 `Consent`, Background and Context). R4 states the base in `policyRule` (`OPTIN` or `OPTOUT`) and nests exceptions as `provision` with a required `type` below the root; R5 states it in `decision` and makes every provision an exception to its parent. A directive with its exceptions has that shape.

* *Export.* A directive with no scope is the base, and its exceptions are the exception provisions. A directive with a scope is written as a base of the opposite effect, its scope as the one exception provision, and its own exceptions nested in that one. FHIR then states the opposite effect outside the scope, where the scheme decides by `_default_access_` and the other directives.
* *Import.* A `Consent` whose exception provisions nest no further is one directive. A `Consent` whose exception provisions nest one level further gives one directive per exception provision. A provision nested deeper is refused with the element named.

== IHE BPPC

BPPC records that a patient acknowledged a policy the policy domain publishes, and carries no structured rule (ITI TF-1 19.1.1). Each `documentationOf/serviceEvent` of the acknowledgement names one policy by `code` and `codeSystem` and gives its time range in `effectiveTime` (ITI TF-3 5.1.2.2.6, 5.1.2.2.7). A system maps each service event to the `ACCESS_POLICY._catalogue_` entry with that identifier: the entry's directive is copied into the subject's settings with `effectiveTime` as `_time_validity_`, the policy identifier in `_identifiers_` and the acknowledgement as `_evidence_`. A policy the catalogue does not hold is refused. Export writes one acknowledgement per distinct `_time_validity_`.

== IHE APPC

An APPC Privacy Consent Document is an XACML 2.0 policy set (APPC ITI TF-3 5.6.2.1.1). XACML places `Effect` on `Rule` alone (XACML 2.0 §5.29, §5.30), so a policy maps to one directive whose `_effect_` is that of its base rule, and a narrower rule of the opposite effect maps to an exception where the policy's rule-combining algorithm lets it decide (deny-overrides for a permit base, permit-overrides for a deny base, first-applicable when the exceptions come first; XACML 2.0 Appendix C). Directives combine across an EHR as the XACML policy-combining deny-overrides (C.1), so a policy set under another policy-combining algorithm is refused. The APPC subject, resource and action attributes map to `_applies_to_`, `_data_` and `_operations_`; a reference to a foundational policy resolves against the catalogue.

== Controller Rules

A rule of `ACCESS_POLICY._rules_` belongs to the controller, not to the subject, so it is never exported as a `Consent`: R5 leaves which "organizational roles have access to what kinds of resources" out of the `Consent` resource (R5 `Consent` §6.2.3).

* *FHIR R5 `Permission`* (https://hl7.org/fhir/R5/permission.html, Trial Use): a policy is one `Permission` with `combining` `deny-overrides`, and each rule one `Permission.rule` whose `type` is the rule's `_effect_`. `_applies_to_` maps to `rule.activity.actor`; a role scope is a `Group` of the performers of that role, because `actor` is a reference only. `_operations_` and `_purposes_` map to `rule.activity.action` and `rule.activity.purpose`, `_data.sensitivities_` to `rule.data.security`, `_data.recorded_` to `rule.data.period`, and `_legal_grounds_` to `Permission.justification.basis`. An exception is a `rule` of the opposite `type` whose activity is narrower. R4 has no `Permission`, so R4 exchange carries no rule.
* *IHE APPC foundational policy*: a rule is one XACML `Rule` of a policy the policy domain publishes, its subject matched on `urn:oasis:names:tc:xacml:2.0:subject:role` for a role scope (APPC ITI TF-3 5.6.2.1.4.6), under the policy-combining deny-overrides (XACML 2.0 Appendix C.1).

== Care Relationships

HL7 models the relationship between a patient and a professional as a care provision act (the ActClass `PCPR`, "taking on whole or partial responsibility for, or attention to, safety and well-being of a subject of care"), and the professional's part in it as a participation type, the code system of the `care_role` default. The sources below map to a care relationship with the role shown; the provider is the organisation the source system acts for, and the period comes from the source.

[cols="2,3", options="header"]
|===
|Source |Care relationship

|HL7 v2 `PV1-7` attending doctor, `PV1-9` consulting doctor, `PV1-8` referring doctor, `PV1-17` admitting doctor
|role `ATND`, `CON`, `REF`, `ADM`; the period of the visit

|HL7 v2 `ROL-3` (table 0443) and `ROL-5`, `ROL-6`
|`AT` to `ATND`, `CP` to `CON`, `RP` to `REF`, `AD` to `ADM`, `PP` and `FHCP` to `RESP`; the period from Role Begin and End Date/Time

|HL7 v2 `PRT-4` role of participation
|the participation type it carries; the period from `PRT-11` and `PRT-12`

|HL7 v2 `PD1-4` patient primary care provider (v2.5 and earlier; withdrawn in v2.9.1)
|role `RESP`, an open period where the policy allows one

|FHIR `Patient.generalPractitioner`
|role `RESP`, target the practitioner or the organisation

|FHIR `EpisodeOfCare.careManager`, `.team` (R4), `.careTeam` (R5)
|`RESP` for the care manager, the team as a `team` target for the rest; period from the episode, and `episode` its `identifier`

|FHIR `Encounter.episodeOfCare` (R4, R5)
|no relationship: a composition the encounter documents is placed in each episode's `identifier`

|HL7 v2 `PV1-19` visit number, `PV1-50` alternate visit id
|the `episode` of the relationships the visit's doctors give, and the placement of the compositions of the visit

|FHIR `Encounter.participant`
|role from `type` (R4 binds the same participation types), period from `period` or from the encounter

|FHIR `CareTeam.participant`
|a membership of the team's `GROUP`, `period` its validity

|FHIR `RelatedPerson` with a `relationship` of `HL7_RoleCode`
|a `related_to` kinship, or a `represents` relationship where the code is a basis of representation
|===

Where `HL7_ParticipationFunction` refines a role (`PCP`, "the healthcare provider that holds primary responsibility for the overall care of a patient"), a jurisdiction may add the code to its `care_role` value set; the default maps it to `RESP`.

== Confidentiality

`CONFIDENTIALITY_MARKING._confidentiality_` is an `HL7_Confidentiality` code, and FHIR `meta.security` and the IHE XDS `confidentialityCode` carry the same code system, so a record's marking and an object's label map to and from them with no table. An export of a record marked above `N` carries the marking on every resource or document it writes. FHIR R5 `Permission` (trial use) is no mapping target; `Consent` stays the exchange form for directives.

== Access Log

An access admitted on a care relationship is projected to a FHIR `AuditEvent` (IHE BALP) with the relationship's role as an `agent.role` coding beside the professional's structural roles, and `agent.policy` naming the `ACCESS_POLICY` version and the rule that named the relationship.
