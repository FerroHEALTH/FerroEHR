openEHR gives no concrete settings class, no consent model, no home for a policy that holds for every EHR of a controller, no code for a care relationship or a professional's part in it, no confidentiality level for a record, and no authorisation meaning for a demographic `CAPABILITY`. This package supplies all but the last. The last stays outside the RM: a system documents the codes its capabilities carry.

== Legal Requirements

Regulation (EU) 2025/327 (EHDS) gives the natural person rights that an access control scheme in the Union must carry:

* *Art 8:* the right to restrict the access of health professionals and healthcare providers to all or parts of the person's data, where the fact of the restriction is not visible to healthcare providers.
* *Art 11(5):* access to restricted data where necessary to protect the vital interests of the person, logged.
* *Art 10:* where a Member State's law gives it, the right to opt out from access through the electronic health data access services, reversible, with Art 10(2) admitting access for vital interests where that law allows it.
* *Art 4(2):* authorisation of other natural persons, for a limited or unlimited period and if needed for a specific purpose only, and the acts of legal representatives under national law.
* *Annex II 3.4:* access rights that take into account the origins and categories of the data.
* *Art 11(1):* health professionals have access to the data "of natural persons under their treatment". The Regulation defines neither the treatment nor a professional's part in it, so the relationship is a fact a system holds, and the vocabulary for it comes from the jurisdiction.
* *Art 11(3) and 11(4):* Member States lay down the categories of data accessible by categories of health professionals or for healthcare tasks, and the rules of the Member State of treatment apply. A national rule is a profile of the controller's policy, never a step of the procedure.
* *Art 11(2) and Art 23:* cross-border access runs through MyHealth@EU, where a foreign professional holds no relationship stored in the system and its credential attests the treatment context.
* *Annex II 3.2:* the access log records the healthcare provider, so a relationship names the provider in whose care it is.

Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (GDPR) Art 4(15) and Art 9 make data concerning health special categories of data. That a person is under the care of a given professional or service is such data, so reading a relationship, a caseload or a list of who is in whose care is an access to each subject it reveals, and is decided and logged as one.

Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (GDPR) Art 6(1) and 9(2) name the grounds of the processing. Care runs on Art 9(2)(h); explicit consent (Art 9(2)(a)) and vital interests (Art 9(2)(c)) are other grounds a directive or an override can rest on, so the model records the legal ground of each directive.

ISO 22600-1 and -2:2014 describe, by their published scope, policy agreements between domains and formal policy, role, authorisation and delegation models. The package names its three sets of facts in those terms: the subject's policy per EHR, the controller's policy, and the role and delegation model in the demographic service.
