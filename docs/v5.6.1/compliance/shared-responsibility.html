FerroEHR Documentation

Shared responsibility

Which obligation under EU and national health-data law the software can carry, and which stays with the organisation that deploys it, is drawn once for the EHR system FerroEHR and FerroBRIDGE form together. The tables, one per law (the GDPR, the EHDS, the CRA, and the national sections for the Netherlands, Germany and Switzerland), and the account of when Cadasto B.V. is also your processor, are published in the FerroHEALTH book: The EHDS EHR system: shared responsibility.

This page keeps what is particular to a FerroEHR deployment.

What a FerroEHR deployment sends out by default

The one thing a FerroEHR deployment sends out by default is the usage report: a random instance id, the version, the licence grant type and coarse performance figures, with no patient data. Cadasto B.V. receives it as controller for its own purposes, and its collector also stores the IP address each report comes from. The usage report page says how to turn it off.

EHDS Chapter II, right by right

EHDS Annex II point 1.3 asks that an EHR system “be designed and developed in such a way that its interoperability, safety and security features uphold the rights of natural persons, in line with the intended purpose of the EHR system, as set out in Chapter II”. A person exercises most of those rights through the electronic health data access services each Member State establishes (Art. 4(1)) and the health professional access services (Art. 12). FerroEHR is the repository those services read and write, so for each provision the table names three things: the part FerroEHR provides, with the route or setting that provides it; what you, the deploying organisation, or the Member State serve or decide; and what a clinical data repository does not do, with the reason.

A row says “shipped” where the tracker issue that delivered the part is closed and “planned” where it is open. Nothing in the table says that a deployment upholds a right: that depends on the access services around the repository and on how you configure it.

Where FerroEHR documents its side

The middle column of each table in the FerroHEALTH book, what the software provides, points at FerroEHR’s own pages:

  • Compliance overview: the legal sources, and what FerroEHR ships, act by act and country by country, with the tracker issue for each control.
  • Control matrix: the live status of every declared control, generated from the tracker.
  • Security and the threat model: how each control is configured, and the risk that survives it.
  • Audit trail: what the access log records and how to read it back.
  • Retention, restriction and objection: the retention register, the restriction of processing and the research objection.
  • Data protection impact assessment, records of processing and the go-live checklist: the companion guidance for the controller’s own documents.
  • Intended purpose: the security environment FerroEHR assumes the deployment provides.

None of these pages tells you whether your deployment satisfies an obligation. That depends on your legal basis, your organisation, your infrastructure and your operating practice, none of which a supplier can see.

The delay of the person’s access (EHDS Art. 3(3))

Art. 3(3) lets a Member State delay a person’s access to their newly registered data until a health professional has explained it. The software part is FerroEHR’s: the hold, the release by a professional, the limit that ends every hold, and the access record of each. The rule is yours to carry: whether your Member State provides for the delay at all, which categories of data it covers, how long a hold may last, and which professional releases a result and when. FerroEHR ships the delay off; see delayed access by the person.
